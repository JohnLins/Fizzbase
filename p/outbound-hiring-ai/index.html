Why outbound hiring beats inbound (and why unemployed candidates often aren’t the best)

Inbound hiring—posting jobs and waiting for applicants—is easy to run but skews toward people who are actively looking. That often means people between roles, underutilized in their current job, or struggling to get traction. The best candidates are usually already employed: they're succeeding where they are, not scrolling job boards. They won't apply to your posting; you have to find them and reach out. That's outbound hiring: identifying and contacting passive talent who match your bar. Fizzbase is built for exactly that—AI-powered people search so you can describe the hire you want and get a list of people who fit, whether or not they're on the market.

The problem with inbound-only hiring

Relying only on inbound means you're selecting from a biased pool: the unemployed, the unhappy, and the underemployed. You miss the high performers who are busy shipping and wouldn't think to apply. Outbound flips the logic: you define the profile (e.g. senior engineers who've scaled systems at high-growth startups, or PMs who've owned 0→1 products at Series A companies), find those people, and approach them. You're not hoping the right person applies—you're going to them.
